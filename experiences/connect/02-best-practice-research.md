# Settled answers for a listing exchange and inbound record intake

**Intake is one pipeline with a run record per arrival.** The stages an arriving record needs (parse, map to fields, validate, write through operations, report per record) are the same whether it came from a spreadsheet, a partner's POST, an email or a scheduled pull; only arrival differs. That is already the design of [`ingest/`](../../coredata/ingest/00-ingest.md), and it is why Connect contributes doors over a pipeline: a source-specific path re-earns retry, idempotency, partial-failure reporting and observability for every new door, exactly as legacy did four times.

**Entity resolution is two phases, and the second one is scored.** The settled shape is a cheap blocking key that narrows candidates, then a comparison over several attributes that yields a score, then a threshold that decides automatic merge, review, or no match. Legacy has the first phase and stops there, treating a single derived address string as proof of identity; the consequence is that a false positive merges two properties and a false negative duplicates one, with no record either way. Scoring makes the middle outcome ("probably the same, ask someone") expressible at all; the middle outcome is the common case for extracted addresses.

**A resolution decision is recorded explicitly.** Whichever way the threshold falls, the pair, the score, the inputs and the verdict are a row, because the alternative is a system whose merges cannot be audited, reversed, or improved from feedback. This also removes the class of repair legacy performs with migrations: a wrong merge is corrected by reversing a recorded decision; no DDL that deletes rows is needed.

**Extracted data is a proposal until a person or an authorised agent accepts it.** Human-in-the-loop review of machine extraction is the permanent design (not a transitional safeguard), because an extractor's confidence is not calibrated across sources and a wrong value written directly is indistinguishable from a value someone entered. Neuro already has the mechanism: `proposed_values` stores candidates from extraction and agents, and nothing reaches `field_values` except through the normal write path when accepted ([`coredata/entity-fields/04-data-model.md`](../../coredata/entity-fields/04-data-model.md)). Connect therefore needs no review queue of its own, and no "AI enhancement status" on the record.

**A vendor push subscription needs a lifecycle owner and a reconciler, because push alone loses messages.** Every major mailbox and calendar API expires its subscriptions, requires renewal ahead of expiry, and drops notifications during outages and re-authorisation; the standard arrangement is a scheduled renewer, a stored cursor for delta reads, and a periodic reconciliation pass that catches what push missed. Legacy has all three pieces and they are correct in outline (a renewal cron, a stored sync cursor, a reconciling sweep); this is an assertion about where they live; they are still needed.

**Acquisition and interpretation are separate layers, and the boundary is the payload.** Integration platforms converge on the same split: a connector per provider stores the credential, the session, the cursor and the vendor's quirks, and a shared pipeline behind it does the parsing, mapping and validation that every source needs identically. The reason is churn. A provider changes its authorization flow, its subscription model or its page structure on its own schedule, and a pipeline that contains that code changes with it. Keeping the pipeline free of provider clients means a new door costs a package; the machinery every other door depends on stays unchanged.

**Inbound-mail idempotency keys on the message identifier over a bounded window, and that identifier is never the record's key.** `Message-ID` is stable for one message and is the right idempotency key; it is supplied by the sender's client, so it is not trustworthy as identity and not unique across the forwarding and mailbox paths without normalisation. The bound matters as much as the key: an unbounded dedup window makes a legitimate re-send months later undeliverable, and no window at all makes every re-forward a duplicate record.

**Fetching a URL a third party supplied is a security decision before it is a parsing one.** A crawl of an arbitrary link needs the URL validated against private address ranges and redirect targets re-validated, a declared politeness policy, a content-type and size ceiling, and a distinction between "the site refused" and "the crawl failed" so the two get different retry treatment. Legacy makes that distinction and folds a bot-wall refusal into a refusal status deliberately (`essos/lib/models/listing_url_ingestion.rb:3-4`), which is the right call; what it lacks is any of it being configurable, and the merge of what it finds being reviewable.

**Cross-tenant visibility is an explicit, default-closed grant, and the record keeps its own tenancy.** The settled pattern for a record one party owns and others may read is an explicit grant plus row-level enforcement, with the owner's tenancy unchanged; it is not a record with no owner that everyone can see unless marked otherwise. The distinction decides what happens on the paths nobody designs: an unauthenticated read, a missing tenant context, a support session, an export. Default-open answers all four wrongly at once. Here the grant is a publication's audience rule ([`coreservices/syndication/04-proposed-model.md`](../../coreservices/syndication/04-proposed-model.md)), which adds field-level disclosure to the same guarantee.

**A consumer's copy of someone else's record is a snapshot with a live link.** The settled shape for syndicated content — the one every listing exchange, data marketplace and shared-catalogue product converges on — separates three things that a naive design conflates: the provider's values, which the consumer may read and never write; the consumer's own additions, which the provider never sees and which survive the provider withdrawing; and the link between them, which carries updates forward and can be broken. A pure view gives the consumer nowhere to put their own work. A pure copy goes stale with no indication and gives the provider no way to correct it. The snapshot-with-a-subscription lets a consumer treat an adopted listing as a native record (commenting, attaching, running agents, creating a deal from it) while the provider keeps ownership of the facts.

**Withdrawal freezes the consumer's copy; it does not delete it.** When a provider unpublishes or narrows an audience, what stops is discovery and the flow of updates. The consumer's annotations, edges and workflow history are the consumer's own data and were earned by their work, so removing them is a data loss the provider has no standing to cause. Marking the copy stale with a date makes the state legible instead of wrong with no indication.

**What does not apply.**

| Technique a reader would expect | Why it is not paid for here |
|---|---|
| Content-addressable storage, or dedup by payload hash | The same offering arrives as different bytes every time — a re-sent email, a re-generated feed row, a page whose markup changed. Hashing the payload detects a duplicate transmission, which the message identifier already does more cheaply, and never detects a duplicate property |
| A dedicated search cluster | [`search/`](../../coreservices/search/00-search.md) measured Postgres as sufficient for filter, sort and text matching across the record surface. Legacy's OpenSearch index exists because the monolith's MySQL could not do it, and it brings a second store to keep consistent with no outbox |
| CQRS, or a purpose-built read model for the screener | The projections in [`coredata/entity-fields/06-reads-projections-views.md`](../../coredata/entity-fields/06-reads-projections-views.md) already are the read model, and they are maintained by machinery that exists |
| A streaming platform for intake volume | [`async/`](../../coreservices/async/00-async.md) settles the bus and its three drivers. Inbound deal flow is not a volume problem; it is a correctness and review problem |
| A trained record-linkage model | A blocking key plus a scored comparison over a handful of address and identity attributes covers this domain, and the training data would be the very review decisions the system has not yet collected. Revisit when the recorded decisions exist — they are the corpus |
| MIME normalisation, HTML-to-text extraction, or an email parsing library in this stack | Conversion owns the transport shape ([`ingest/`](../../coredata/ingest/00-ingest.md)); Connect receives a parsed arrival. Legacy's formatter is an acquisition concern that happens to live in the service |
| A rules engine for the buy-box fit an agent evaluates | Fit is a predicate over fields, which [`views/`](../views/00-views.md) and the field system already express, and an agent reasoning over a record needs no engine of its own |
